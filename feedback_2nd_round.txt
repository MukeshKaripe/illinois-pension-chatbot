## 1. The biggest issue: §11-130 is being dismissed too easily

Your chatbot says:

> “Section 11-130(1) does not control because it provides an annuity from accumulated employee and city contributions, and the actuarial conversion factors needed to compute that annuity are not provided.”

This reasoning is questionable.

The actual §11-130 says that an employee age 55–59 with 10+ years who withdraws is entitled to an annuity, and for 20+ years it specifies that the age-and-service annuity comes from the accumulated employee and city contributions, plus prior-service annuity for a present employee. It also explicitly says the annuity is computed as of the employee's age at withdrawal. 

So your agent shouldn't conclude:

```text
§11-130
   ↓
conversion factors unavailable
   ↓
ignore §11-130
```

It should instead reason:

```text
§11-130 is applicable
        ↓
What benefit does §11-130 produce?
        ↓
Can the supplied employee data determine it?
        ↓
YES → calculate
NO  → identify missing accumulation/conversion data
```

And now you've actually supplied:

* Employee accumulation = $180,000
* City accumulation = $360,000
* Prior service = $0

So **your test is specifically designed to see whether the agent can use §11-130**, not simply bypass it because §11-134 has a convenient percentage formula.

This is the biggest thing I'd investigate.

# 2. The agent is choosing §11-134 because it can calculate it

This sentence is especially concerning:

> “The tiered 1.80%/2.00%/2.20%/2.40% election in Section 11-134(a) is not used because the applicable 2.40% election produces the larger annuity.”

That is backwards from a legal reasoning perspective.

The system shouldn't do:

```text
find applicable formulas
       ↓
calculate all possible numbers
       ↓
pick the largest
```

unless the statute **explicitly grants the employee that choice**.

The source itself says that certain §11-134 provisions are elections “in lieu of any other employee annuity provided in this Section.” 

So the agent needs to distinguish:

### Statutory choice

> The law explicitly allows the member to elect between alternatives.

from:

### Model optimization

> “This formula produces a larger pension, therefore we'll use it.”

Those are **not the same thing**.

Your recipe representation should therefore contain something like:

```json
{
  "decision_type": "statutory_election",
  "election_required": true,
  "options": [
    "...",
    "..."
  ],
  "selection_rule": "member_election"
}
```

rather than allowing the LLM to choose `max(benefit_options)`.

==================== feedback for overall structure of all the responses ==================
For your pension chatbot, I would **not** use one universal response format. A legal explanation, a calculation, a missing-input situation, and a multi-scenario comparison should each have a slightly different presentation.

The important thing is that the user should be able to answer these questions within a few seconds:

> **What applies to me?**
> **Why does it apply?**
> **What do I need to provide / what did you assume?**
> **What is the result?**
> **Where does the law say this?**

I would build around the following response structures.

---

# 1. Simple legal question

Example:

> “What does Section 11-116 define as salary?”

### Recommended response

```text
## Section 11-116 — Salary

Section 11-116 defines “salary” primarily as the compensation payable
for normal full-time service, excluding overtime and certain other
payments.

### In simple terms

For pension calculations, salary generally refers to the compensation
that counts under the statutory definition—not simply every payment
shown on a paycheck.

### What it includes

• Regular compensation for normal full-time service
• Certain nonannual compensation converted under §11-217
• Certain lodging/meals when the statutory conditions are met
• Employee contributions picked up by the employer under §11-170.1

### What it excludes

• Overtime
• Final vacation payment
• Other compensation excluded by the statute

### Legal basis

§11-116(a)–(d)

[View supporting provision]
```

### Why this works

The user gets the **plain-English answer first**, then the details.

Don't start with:

> “According to subsection (a)...”

That is legally precise but cognitively poor.

# 2. “Which provision applies to me?”

This is one of the **most important response types** for your system.

Example:

> “I am 56 with 21.5 years of service and currently employed. Which retirement provision applies?”

This should **not immediately jump to a formula**.

### Recommended structure

```text
## Your applicable retirement provisions

Based on the information provided, two statutory pathways need to be
considered.

### 1. §11-130 — Potentially applicable

You are:

• Age: 56
• Service: 21.5 years
• Currently employed

§11-130 covers an employee age 55 or older but younger than 60 who
withdraws from service with at least 10 years of service.

Because you are currently employed, the withdrawal condition has not
yet occurred.

**Status: Potential pathway — becomes applicable upon withdrawal.**

### 2. §11-134 — Also needs evaluation

§11-134 contains separate annuity provisions based on age, service,
and other statutory conditions.

**Status: Requires branch/election evaluation.**

### What this means

You cannot be told that one formula automatically controls solely from
your age and service.

The system must determine:

1. Whether you withdraw now
2. Which §11-134 branch applies, if any
3. Whether the provisions operate as alternatives, minimums, or
elections
4. Whether any age-based reduction applies

### Current conclusion

**You are not yet at a point where a single retirement formula can be
selected solely from the information provided.**

I can calculate the applicable pathways once the withdrawal date and
remaining required inputs are established.

### Legal basis

§11-130  
§11-134  
§11-216
```

This format is particularly important because your current agent has demonstrated the exact failure mode you want to prevent:

> “§11-134 is calculable, therefore §11-134 must be the applicable provision.”

Those are **not the same question**.

Your UI should therefore distinguish:

**Applicable provision**
from
**Calculable provision**.

When the user actually asks for a pension calculation, the response should become more structured.

Example:

### Recommended format

```text

### Result

**Estimated annual pension: $32,126.78**

**Estimated monthly pension: $2,677.23**

---

### How the calculation was derived

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Age | 56 |
| Creditable service | 21.5 years |
| Highest average salary | $69,956.40 |
| Applicable provision | §11-134(...) |
| Base percentage | 2.40% per year |
| Age reduction | 11% |
| Final result | $32,126.78 |

### Calculation

Base annuity:

$69,956.40 × 21.5 × 2.40%  
= **$36,097.50**

Age reduction:

$36,097.50 × 11%  
= **$3,971.73**

Reduced annual pension:

$36,097.50 − $3,971.73  
= **$32,125.77**

### Important

This estimate assumes that the applicable statutory pathway,
salary basis, service credit and age-reduction period above are
correct.

### Supporting law

§11-134(...)
§11-216
...
```

There is one issue here: the arithmetic in that example must always be generated by your deterministic engine. The LLM should **never be trusted to perform the arithmetic**.

Instead:

and optionally:

That recipe can be expandable.

```text
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

That consistency will make the bot feel substantially more reliable.

---

# I would also separate “answer” from “evidence”

This is particularly important for your RAG architecture.

Instead of this:

> According to §11-134(a), which states that...

> ...15 lines of statute...

> therefore...

I would use:

### Answer

> Based on your current facts, the applicable pathway is §11-134(...).

### Why

> You satisfy A, B, and C.

### Calculation

> Formula → values → result.

### Evidence

> **§11-134(...)** — supports the eligibility/formula.
> **§11-116** — supports salary definition.
> **§11-216** — supports service-credit calculation.

So retrieval evidence supports the answer without **overwhelming the user with retrieved text**.

# One especially important addition for your system

I would add a compact **“Decision status”** badge/label to calculation responses.

For example:

| Status                          | Meaning                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Calculated**                  | All required conditions and inputs established                       |
| **Calculated with assumptions** | Result is possible, but one or more assumptions materially affect it |
| **Multiple pathways**           | More than one statutory provision may apply                          |
| **Needs information**           | Required input is missing                                            |
| **Unable to determine**         | Evidence does not establish a unique legal pathway                   |

This makes the chatbot much easier to trust.

For example:

> **Status: Multiple statutory pathways**

is far better than giving the user a confident number when your agent hasn't actually resolved §11-130 vs §11-134.

# My preferred “gold standard” calculation response

For your project specifically, I'd aim for something approximately like this:

```text
## Estimated retirement benefit

**Status: Calculated**

### Result

**Annual:** $32,126.78  
**Monthly:** $2,677.23

### Applicable provision

**§11-134(...)**

The provision applies because:

✓ Age requirement satisfied
✓ Service requirement satisfied
✓ Retirement condition satisfied
✓ Required cross-references resolved

### Competing provisions considered

**§11-130** — considered because age is 55–59 and service exceeds
10 years.

**Disposition:** [why it does / does not control, or whether it is
an alternative/election]

### Calculation

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Salary basis | Highest 4-year average | $69,956.40 |
| Service | Creditable service | 21.5 yrs |
| Base benefit | salary × service × factor | $36,097.50 |
| Reduction | applicable age reduction | −$3,971.73 |
| Final annual benefit | base − reduction | **$32,126.78** |

### Inputs used

| Input | Value | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 56 | Employee record |
| Service | 21.5 | Employee record / §11-216 |
| Salary | $69,956.40 | Salary history / §11-116 |
| Retirement date | ... | User |

### Assumptions

• ...
• ...

### Validation

✓ Statutory evidence found  
✓ Applicability conditions checked  
✓ Cross-references resolved  
✓ Required inputs available  
✓ Recipe validated  
✓ Deterministic calculation executed

### Legal basis

§11-134(...)  
§11-116  
§11-216
```

That, in my view, is the **target UX** for the pension chatbot.

The most important change from your current outputs is that the bot should no longer simply say **“I selected §11-134 because I can calculate it.”** It should explicitly show **candidate provisions → applicability → disposition → recipe → calculation**. That makes the system auditable and directly addresses the weakness exposed by your §11-130 / §11-134 tests.

And I would keep the **full machine-readable recipe out of the normal answer**, with a UI expansion such as **“View calculation recipe”** for debugging/audit users.
